Signal-agnostic model validation relies exclusively on background data. Since no unsupervised method can guarantee optimal future anomaly detection for arbitrary anomalies, a necessary assumption is that future anomalies must be deviations along a latent direction that is learnable from typical data. This assumption can be formalized as a monotone likelihood-ratio condition.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Latent likelihood-ratio recoverability]
\label{ass:lrr}
Let \(P_0\) denote the typical distribution and \(P_1\) a future anomaly distribution. There exists a scalar latent coordinate
\[
    Z=T(X)
\]
and a nondecreasing function \(h\) such that
\[
    \frac{dP_1}{dP_0}(x)=h(T(x)).
\]
Thus larger values of \(Z\) are more anomalous in the Neyman--Pearson sense. In addition, the two typical validation domains are treated as noisy samples of the same latent structure: conditioned on \(Z\), they differ only in the randomness entailed by the sampling process.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:lrr} formalizes the standard anomaly-detection premise: a good model learns a coordinate of typicality, and anomalies are detected as departures along that coordinate. Then, the goal of a model validation criterion is not to discover arbitrary unseen anomalies, but to select, among trained candidate detectors, the score that recovers this latent anomaly-relevant coordinate most reliably.

In our setting, trained anomaly detectors already map inputs to scalar scores.
We therefore formulate a closed-form version of Assumption~\ref{ass:lrr}
directly at the score level, modelling each detector as a noisy measurement of
a latent anomaly-relevant coordinate, possibly together with stable nuisance
structure.

\begin{assumption}[Gaussian score channel with stable nuisance]
\label{ass:channel}
Let \(\widetilde S_\phi\) denote an anomaly score produced
by a candidate detector \(\phi\), assuming that larger values are
more anomalous. Under typical data, the two validation samples satisfy
\[
    \widetilde S_\phi^{(d)}
    =
    c_\phi
    +
    a_\phi Z
    +
    b_\phi U_\phi
    +
    \sigma_\phi \varepsilon_\phi^{(d)},
    \qquad d\in\{1,2\},
\]
where
\[
    Z,U_\phi,\varepsilon_\phi^{(1)},\varepsilon_\phi^{(2)}
    \stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim}\Normal(0,1),
    \qquad
    a_\phi\ge 0,
    \qquad
    b_\phi\in\R,
    \qquad
    \sigma_\phi\ge 0,
    \qquad
    a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2+\sigma_\phi^2>0 .
\]

The coefficient \(a_\phi\) measures sensitivity to the anomaly-relevant
coordinate \(Z\). The coefficient \(b_\phi\) measures sensitivity to a
reproducible nuisance coordinate \(U_\phi\) that is stable across validation
views but is not anomaly-relevant. The term
\(\sigma_\phi \varepsilon_\phi^{(d)}\) captures view-specific noise. The
standardized scores are:

\[
    S_\phi^{(d)}
    =
    \frac{\widetilde S_\phi^{(d)}-c_\phi}
    {\sqrt{a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2+\sigma_\phi^2}} .
\]
Let
\[
    \rho_{\phi}^{Z}
    =
    \frac{a_\phi^2}{a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2+\sigma_\phi^2},
    \qquad
    \rho_{\phi}^{R}
    =
    \frac{a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2}{a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2+\sigma_\phi^2}.
\]
Here \(\rho_{\phi}^{Z}\) is the anomaly-aligned fraction of score variance,
while
\[
    \rho_{\phi}^{R}
    =
    \Corr(S_\phi^{(1)},S_\phi^{(2)})
\]
is the total reproducible fraction of score variance.

As per our initial assumption, future anomalies are positive shifts of the same latent coordinate:
\[
    Z\mid Y=1\sim\Normal(\delta,1),
    \qquad
    \delta>0,
\]
while \(U_\phi\) and \(\varepsilon_\phi^{(d)}\) retain their typical-data laws.
Consequently, after the same typical-data normalisation,
\[
    S_\phi\mid Y=0\sim\Normal(0,1),
    \qquad
    S_\phi\mid Y=1\sim\Normal(\delta\sqrt{\rho_\phi^Z},1).
\]
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:channel} separates anomaly-aligned reliability
\(\rho_\phi^Z\) from total reproducible reliability \(\rho_\phi^R\). The two
coincide only when the stable nuisance component is absent; that is, when \(b_\phi=0\). A detector that is
useful for anomaly detection should place reproducible score variation on
\(Z\), increasing \(a_\phi\), rather than on nuisance structure \(U_\phi\),
reducing \(b_\phi\). A detector can therefore look reproducible on typical data
while still being poorly aligned with future anomalies if its reproducible
variation is dominated by \(U_\phi\).

\begin{lemma}[TPR is controlled by anomaly-aligned reliability]
\label{lem:tpr-rhoz}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:channel}, for every false-positive rate
\(\alpha\in(0,1)\),
\[
    \TPR_\phi(\alpha)
    =
    \Phi\!\left(
        \delta\sqrt{\rho_\phi^Z}-\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)
    \right).
\]
Hence \(\TPR_\phi(\alpha)\) is strictly increasing in
\(\rho_\phi^Z\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Under typical data, \(S_\phi\sim\Normal(0,1)\), so the FPR-\(\alpha\)
threshold is \(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\). Under anomalies,
\[
    S_\phi\mid Y=1
    \sim
    \Normal(\delta\sqrt{\rho_\phi^Z},1).
\]
The stated expression follows by evaluating the anomaly exceedance probability
at that threshold.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducible reliability and marginal non-identifiability}

We now compare how CAP and marginal stability criteria behave with respect to
the reliability parameters in Assumption~\ref{ass:channel}. CAP is evaluated on
paired validation scores, so its population objective can vary with the total
reproducible reliability \(\rho_\phi^R\). Wasserstein distance and threshold
drift only compare the two validation score histograms. They can measure
marginal score drift between validation domains, but they discard the paired
dependence that defines \(\rho_\phi^R\).

\subsection{CAP monotonicity}

For a binary Gibbs posterior, let
\[
    q_\beta(s)=\sigma(\beta s)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\beta s}},
    \qquad
    m_\beta(s)=2q_\beta(s)-1=\tanh\!\left(\frac{\beta s}{2}\right).
\]
The approximation capacity kernel for a pair \((s_1,s_2)\) is
\[
    \log\{q_\beta(s_1)q_\beta(s_2)+[1-q_\beta(s_1)][1-q_\beta(s_2)]\}
    =
    -\log 2+
    \log\{1+m_\beta(s_1)m_\beta(s_2)\}.
\]
Since the constant \(-\log 2\) is irrelevant for model selection, let \(L_\phi\) be the optimization objective
\[
    L_\phi(\beta)
    =
    \E\log\{1+m_\beta(S_\phi^{(1)})m_\beta(S_\phi^{(2)})\}
\]
and \(L_\phi^\star\) denote the CAP lift:
\[
    L_\phi^\star
    =
    \sup_{\beta\in[0,B]} L_\phi(\beta),
    \qquad B>0.
\]

\begin{lemma}[CAP is monotone in paired Gaussian reliability]
\label{lem:price}
Let \((U,V)\) be standard bivariate Gaussian with correlation \(\rho\in[0,1)\). For every \(\beta>0\),
\[
    C_\beta(\rho)
    =
    \E_\rho\log\{1+m_\beta(U)m_\beta(V)\}
\]
is strictly increasing in \(\rho\). Consequently, \(\sup_{\beta\in[0,B]} C_\beta(\rho)\) is strictly increasing in \(\rho\) on \([0,1]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
    H_\beta(u,v)=\log\{1+m_\beta(u)m_\beta(v)\}.
\]
Price's theorem gives
\[
    \frac{d}{d\rho}\E_\rho H_\beta(U,V)
    =
    \E_\rho\left[\frac{\partial^2 H_\beta}{\partial u\,\partial v}(U,V)\right].
\]
Since \(m_\beta'(t)=(\beta/2)\sech^2(\beta t/2)>0\),
\[
    \frac{\partial^2 H_\beta}{\partial u\,\partial v}(u,v)
    =
    \frac{m_\beta'(u)m_\beta'(v)}{
        \{1+m_\beta(u)m_\beta(v)\}^2
    }
    >0.
\]
Therefore \(C_\beta(\rho)\) is strictly increasing for every fixed \(\beta>0\). The optimized objective is increasing as a supremum of increasing functions. It is strict because for \(\rho>0\), a small-\(\beta\) expansion gives
\[
    C_\beta(\rho)=\frac{\beta^2}{4}\rho+O(\beta^4),
\]
so the maximizing \(\beta\) is nonzero whenever \(\rho>0\); applying the fixed-\(\beta\) strict monotonicity at such a maximizer gives strict increase. The case \(\rho=1\) follows by continuity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CAP is monotone in reproducible reliability]
\label{thm:score}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:channel}, the optimized CAP lift \(L_\phi^\star\)
is strictly increasing in \(\rho_\phi^R\). Therefore, for any candidate family
\(\mathcal A\),
\[
    \argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal A} L_\phi^\star
    =
    \argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal A} \rho_\phi^R,
\]
with equality of maximizer sets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:channel}, \((S_\phi^{(1)},S_\phi^{(2)})\) is a
standard bivariate Gaussian pair with correlation \(\rho_\phi^R\). Therefore
\[
    L_\phi^\star
    =
    \sup_{\beta\in[0,B]} C_\beta(\rho_\phi^R).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:price} implies that this quantity is strictly increasing in
\(\rho_\phi^R\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[CAP--TPR alignment with stable nuisance]
\label{cor:cap-tpr-alignment}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:channel}, let \(\mathcal A\) be a candidate family
and define the anomaly alignment of the reproducible component by
\[
    \pi_\phi
    =
    \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{a_\phi^2}{a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2},
        & a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2>0,\\[1.2ex]
    0,  & a_\phi^2+b_\phi^2=0.
    \end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
    \rho_\phi^Z=\pi_\phi\rho_\phi^R.
\]
Let
\[
    \mathcal A_R^\star
    =
    \argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal A}\rho_\phi^R .
\]
Every CAP maximizer is TPR-optimal at every false-positive rate
\(\alpha\in(0,1)\) if and only if
\[
    \forall \phi^\star\in\mathcal A_R^\star,\ \forall \psi\in\mathcal A,
    \qquad
    \pi_{\phi^\star}\rho_{\phi^\star}^R
    \ge
    \pi_\psi\rho_\psi^R .
    \tag{A}
    \label{eq:alignment-condition}
\]
Equivalently, every maximizer of total reproducible reliability must also
maximize the anomaly-aligned reliability \(\rho_\phi^Z\). A simple sufficient condition for~\eqref{eq:alignment-condition} is monotone
alignment:
\[
    \rho_\phi^R\ge \rho_\psi^R
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \pi_\phi\ge \pi_\psi
    \qquad
    \forall\phi,\psi\in\mathcal A.
    \tag{B}
    \label{eq:monotone-alignment}
\]
In coefficient form, this states that more reproducible detectors must not have a
larger stable-nuisance ratio,
\[
    \frac{b_\phi^2}{a_\phi^2}
    \le
    \frac{b_\psi^2}{a_\psi^2},
\]
with the convention that the ratio is \(+\infty\) when \(a_\phi=0\) and
\(b_\phi\ne0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The identity \(\rho_\phi^Z=\pi_\phi\rho_\phi^R\) follows directly from the
definitions. Theorem~\ref{thm:score} shows that CAP maximizes
\(\rho_\phi^R\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:tpr-rhoz} shows that TPR is strictly increasing
in \(\rho_\phi^Z\). Therefore, every CAP maximizer is TPR-optimal if and only if
every \(\rho_\phi^R\)-maximizer also maximizes
\(\rho_\phi^Z=\pi_\phi\rho_\phi^R\), which is exactly
\eqref{eq:alignment-condition}. Finally,
\eqref{eq:monotone-alignment} implies \eqref{eq:alignment-condition} because a
maximizer of \(\rho_\phi^R\) also has maximal \(\pi_\phi\), and hence maximal
product \(\pi_\phi\rho_\phi^R\).
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:cap-tpr-alignment} should be read as an alignment condition,
not as an assumption that nuisance is absent. CAP rewards reproducible score
variation. In the score channel, reproducible variation has an anomaly-relevant
part, \(a_\phi Z\), and a stable nuisance part, \(b_\phi U_\phi\). The ratio
\[
    \frac{b_\phi^2}{a_\phi^2}
\]
measures how much stable nuisance signal the score contains relative to true
anomaly signal. Thus, the sufficient condition says that a detector with larger
reproducible reliability should not become more stable for the wrong reason. Its
reproducible variation should be at least as anomaly-aligned.

The alignment condition follows the contract between training and validation: training must produce scores whose reproducible
variation is intended to encode typicality, and CAP then selects the candidate in which that learned structure is most reproducible rather than merely
lowest-loss. If the condition fails,
CAP can still select a highly reproducible score, but that score may be
reproducible because it has learned stable nuisance structure. In a perfect
detector, all reproducible variation is anomaly-relevant (i.e. \(b_\phi=0\)) and CAP is automatically aligned with TPR.

\subsection{Marginal stability non-identifiability}

We showed that CAP is aligned with \(\rho_\phi^R\), and derived conditions under
which this alignment also implies alignment with \(\rho_\phi^Z\) and thus with TPR.
In this section we will show that marginal stability criteria cannot satisfy this requirement
altogether, as \(\rho_\phi^R\) is a property of the joint distribution of the two validation scores,
whereas marginal stability criteria only depend on the marginal score distributions \(F_{\phi,1}\) and \(F_{\phi,2}\).

\begin{definition}[Marginal stability criteria]
\label{def:marginal-stability}
Let
\[
    F_{\phi,d}(t)=\Pbb(S_\phi^{(d)}\le t),
    \qquad d\in\{1,2\},
\]
be the population CDFs of the score produced by detector \(\phi\) on two
typical validation domains. A validation criterion is
\emph{marginal stability} if there exists a functional \(\Psi\) such that
\[
    M(\phi)=\Psi(F_{\phi,1},F_{\phi,2}).
\]
Equivalently, if two detectors have the same two marginal score laws, then
the criterion assigns them the same value, regardless of their paired
dependence or anomaly-detection power.
\end{definition}

The Wasserstein and threshold drift baselines are marginal stability criteria, as
their population forms are, respectively,
\[
    M_{W_1}(\phi)
    =
    W_1\!\left(
        \cL(S_\phi^{(1)}),
        \cL(S_\phi^{(2)})
    \right)
    =
    \int_0^1
    \left|
        F_{\phi,1}^{-1}(u)-F_{\phi,2}^{-1}(u)
    \right|du,
\]
and, for target FPR \(\alpha\in(0,1)\),
\[
    M_{\mathrm{thr},\alpha}(\phi)
    =
    \left|
        \Pbb\!\left(
            S_\phi^{(2)}\ge F_{\phi,1}^{-1}(1-\alpha)
        \right)-\alpha
    \right|
    =
    \left|
        1-F_{\phi,2}\!\left(F_{\phi,1}^{-1}(1-\alpha)\right)-\alpha
    \right|,
\]
assuming continuity to avoid quantile ties. In contrast, CAP does not belong to
this family because its population objective depends on the joint law of
\((S_\phi^{(1)},S_\phi^{(2)})\).

Marginal stability can measure the amount of domain shift between \(F_1\) and
\(F_2\), but it cannot identify the paired reproducible reliability
\(\rho_\phi^R\). This is the non-identifiability obstruction.

\begin{theorem}[Non-identifiability of reproducible reliability from marginal stability]
\label{thm:marginal-nonidentifiability}
Let \(M\) be a marginal stability criterion. Fix
\(\alpha\in(0,1)\), a validation shift \(\eta\in\R\), and two reproducible
reliability levels
\[
    0\le \rho_{\phi_-}^R<\rho_{\phi_+}^R\le 1.
\]
There exist two candidate detectors \(\phi_-\) and \(\phi_+\) whose validation
score marginals are identical across candidates,
\[
    F_{\phi_+,1}=F_{\phi_-,1}=\Phi,
    \qquad
    F_{\phi_+,2}=F_{\phi_-,2},
    \qquad
    F_{\phi_+,2}(t)=\Phi(t-\eta),
\]
but whose paired reproducible reliabilities are \(\rho_{\phi_+}^R\) and
\(\rho_{\phi_-}^R\). Hence every marginal stability criterion ties the two
candidates,
\[
    M(\phi_+)=M(\phi_-),
\]
even though their reproducible reliabilities are different. In particular,
\[
    M_{W_1}(\phi_+)=M_{W_1}(\phi_-)=|\eta|,
\]
and
\[
    M_{\mathrm{thr},\alpha}(\phi_+)
    =
    M_{\mathrm{thr},\alpha}(\phi_-)
    =
    \left|
        1-\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-\eta\right)-\alpha
    \right|.
\]
When \(\eta=0\), the common marginal stability value is perfect. When
\(\eta\ne0\), the common value is nonzero. In both cases, marginal stability is
non-discriminative over \(\rho_\phi^R\). In the calibrated subcase \(\eta=0\),
if
\[
    \pi_{\phi_+}\rho_{\phi_+}^R
    >
    \pi_{\phi_-}\rho_{\phi_-}^R,
\]
then Corollary~\ref{cor:cap-tpr-alignment} implies that CAP and TPR rank
\(\phi_+\) above \(\phi_-\), whereas every marginal stability criterion ties
the two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each \(\rho\in[0,1]\), let
\[
    G_\rho^{(d)}
    =
    \sqrt{\rho}\,Z
    +
    \sqrt{1-\rho}\,\varepsilon^{(d)},
    \qquad d\in\{1,2\},
\]
where \(Z,\varepsilon^{(1)},\varepsilon^{(2)}\) are independent standard
Gaussian variables. Then
\((G_\rho^{(1)},G_\rho^{(2)})\) is a standard bivariate Gaussian pair with
correlation \(\rho\). Define validation scores by
\[
    S_\rho^{(1)}=G_\rho^{(1)},
    \qquad
    S_\rho^{(2)}=\eta+G_\rho^{(2)}.
\]
Then \(S_\rho^{(1)}\sim\Normal(0,1)\),
\(S_\rho^{(2)}\sim\Normal(\eta,1)\), and
\(\Corr(S_\rho^{(1)},S_\rho^{(2)})=\rho\) for every
\(\rho\). Instantiate \(\phi_-\) with \(\rho=\rho_{\phi_-}^R\) and
\(\phi_+\) with \(\rho=\rho_{\phi_+}^R\). The two candidates have the same
marginal validation laws, so any marginal stability criterion gives them the
same value.

For Wasserstein distance, the two candidate values are both
\[
    W_1(\Normal(0,1),\Normal(\eta,1))=|\eta|.
\]
For threshold drift, the threshold calibrated on the first validation domain is
\(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\). Under the second validation domain, the exceedance
probability is
\[
    1-\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-\eta\right),
\]
which gives the displayed common threshold-drift value. Finally, in the
calibrated subcase \(\eta=0\), the displayed strict inequality is the two-model
form of \(\rho_{\phi_+}^Z>\rho_{\phi_-}^Z\). Since
\(\rho_{\phi_+}^R>\rho_{\phi_-}^R\), Theorem~\ref{thm:score} ranks
\(\phi_+\) above \(\phi_-\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:tpr-rhoz} gives the same TPR
ordering.
\end{proof}

The same obstruction also explains their unreliability as ranking metrics. If
\(\phi_-\) has identical validation marginals and \(\phi_+\) has a location
shift \(F_{\phi_+,1}=\Phi\), \(F_{\phi_+,2}(t)=\Phi(t-\eta)\) with
\(\eta>0\), then smaller-is-better marginal stability criteria prefer
\(\phi_-\). In particular,
\[
    M_{W_1}(\phi_-)=0<M_{W_1}(\phi_+)=\eta,
    \qquad
    M_{\mathrm{thr},\alpha}(\phi_-)=0
    <
    M_{\mathrm{thr},\alpha}(\phi_+),
\]
even if \(\rho_{\phi_-}^R<\rho_{\phi_+}^R\). When additionally
\(\pi_{\phi_+}\rho_{\phi_+}^R>\pi_{\phi_-}\rho_{\phi_-}^R\), this means
preferring the lower-TPR detector.

\section{Gaussian linear anomaly model}

We will now illustrate the previous findings with a Gaussian linear anomaly model
that has an analytical solution. The model
contains an anomaly-relevant latent coordinate, a stable nuisance subspace,
and sample-specific noise.

\begin{definition}[Gaussian linear model with stable nuisance]
\label{def:linear-gaussian}
Let \(w_\star\in\R^p\) be a unit vector and let
\(\lambda_Z\in(0,1]\). Let \(\Gamma_U\succeq0\) be a nuisance covariance
satisfying
\[
    \Gamma_U w_\star=0,
    \qquad
    \lambda_Z w_\star w_\star^\top+\Gamma_U\preceq I.
\]
Choose \(B\) such that \(BB^\top=\Gamma_U\). Under typical data, let the two
validation views be
\[
    X^{(d)}
    =
    \sqrt{\lambda_Z}\,Z w_\star
    +
    B U
    +
    \xi^{(d)},
    \qquad d\in\{1,2\},
\]
where \(Z\sim\Normal(0,1)\), \(U\sim\Normal(0,I)\), and
\[
    \xi^{(d)}
    \stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim}
    \Normal(0,I-\lambda_Z w_\star w_\star^\top-\Gamma_U)
\]
are mutually independent. Future anomalies shift only the anomaly-relevant
coordinate:
\[
    Z\mid Y=1\sim\Normal(\delta,1),
    \qquad \delta>0,
\]
while \(U\) and \(\xi^{(d)}\) keep their typical-data laws.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[CAP recovery of the likelihood-ratio direction]
\label{cor:linear}
In the Gaussian linear model of Definition~\ref{def:linear-gaussian}, consider
unit-norm linear scores
\[
    S_w^{(d)}=w^\top X^{(d)},
    \qquad
    \|w\|=1,
    \qquad
    w^\top w_\star\ge0.
\]
For each \(w\), we can define
\[
    a_w^2=\lambda_Z(w^\top w_\star)^2,
    \qquad
    b_w^2=w^\top\Gamma_U w,
    \qquad
    \sigma_w^2=1-a_w^2-b_w^2,
\]
which aligns the expression of \(S_w^{(d)}\) in terms of Assumption~\ref{ass:channel}, with
\[
    \rho_w^Z=a_w^2,
    \qquad
    \rho_w^R=a_w^2+b_w^2
    =
    w^\top
    (\lambda_Z w_\star w_\star^\top+\Gamma_U)
    w .
\]
The likelihood-ratio anomaly direction is \(w_\star\), and it is the unique
maximizer of \(\TPR_w(\alpha)\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). On any candidate
family \(\mathcal W\), CAP maximizers are TPR-optimal within
\(\mathcal W\) whenever
\[
    \argmax_{w\in\mathcal W}\{a_w^2+b_w^2\}
    \subseteq
    \argmax_{w\in\mathcal W} a_w^2.
\]
This is the linear-Gaussian form of the alignment condition in
Corollary~\ref{cor:cap-tpr-alignment}: a score may contain stable nuisance
power \(b_w^2\), but nuisance power must not make a less anomaly-aligned
direction maximize total reproducible reliability. In particular, over all unit-norm scores with \(w^\top w_\star\ge0\), this
condition is implied by the covariance condition
\[
    \lambda_Z>\|\Gamma_U\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Under this sufficient condition, \(w_\star\) is the unique CAP maximizer:
\[
    \argmax_{\|w\|=1,\,w^\top w_\star\ge0}
    L_w^\star
    =
    \{w_\star\}
    =
    \argmax_{\|w\|=1,\,w^\top w_\star\ge0}
    \TPR_w(\alpha).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For any unit \(w\), the typical marginal covariance of \(X^{(d)}\) is \(I\), so
\(S_w^{(d)}\sim\Normal(0,1)\). Moreover,
\[
    S_w^{(d)}
    =
    \sqrt{\lambda_Z}(w^\top w_\star) Z
    +
    w^\top B U
    +
    w^\top \xi^{(d)}.
\]
The nuisance term \(w^\top B U\) is a scalar Gaussian with variance
\(w^\top\Gamma_Uw=b_w^2\), and the view-specific term has variance
\(\sigma_w^2\). Thus the linear score has exactly the score-channel form with
coefficients \(a_w,b_w,\sigma_w\). Therefore
\[
    \rho_w^Z=a_w^2,
    \qquad
    \rho_w^R=a_w^2+b_w^2.
\]

Under anomalies, the marginal distribution of \(X\) is
\[
    \Normal(\delta\sqrt{\lambda_Z}\,w_\star,I).
\]
The likelihood ratio is therefore
\[
    \log\frac{dP_1}{dP_0}(x)
    =
    \delta\sqrt{\lambda_Z}\,w_\star^\top x
    -
    \frac{\delta^2\lambda_Z}{2},
\]
so the likelihood-ratio direction is \(w_\star\). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:tpr-rhoz},
\[
    \TPR_w(\alpha)
    =
    \Phi\!\left(
        \delta\sqrt{\lambda_Z}(w^\top w_\star)
        -
        \Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)
    \right),
\]
which is uniquely maximized by \(w=w_\star\) under the sign constraint
\(w^\top w_\star\ge0\). The candidate-family statement follows directly from
Corollary~\ref{cor:cap-tpr-alignment}, because CAP maximizes
\(\rho_w^R=a_w^2+b_w^2\), while TPR is strictly increasing in
\(\rho_w^Z=a_w^2\).

Let
\[
    \lambda_U=\|\Gamma_U\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Since \(\Gamma_Uw_\star=0\),
\[
    w^\top\Gamma_Uw
    \le
    \lambda_U\{1-(w^\top w_\star)^2\}.
\]
Hence, if \(\lambda_Z>\lambda_U\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \rho_w^R
    &=
    \lambda_Z(w^\top w_\star)^2
    +
    w^\top\Gamma_Uw  \\
    &\le
    \lambda_U
    +
    (\lambda_Z-\lambda_U)(w^\top w_\star)^2
    \le
    \lambda_Z,
\end{aligned}
\]
with equality only when \(w=w_\star\). Thus \(w_\star\) is the unique maximizer
of \(\rho_w^R\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:score} makes it the unique CAP maximizer.
\end{proof}

The condition \(\lambda_Z>\|\Gamma_U\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) is the covariance form of
the nuisance-ratio condition. A direction \(w\) can have \(b_w^2>0\), but it
cannot be selected by CAP unless its total reproducible power
\(a_w^2+b_w^2\) exceeds that of the anomaly direction. The condition prevents
this by requiring every stable nuisance direction to be less reproducible than
\(w_\star\).

Marginal stability behaves differently because it only sees score histograms. In
the calibrated version of the above model, unit-norm scores behave as standard gaussians.
Therefore Wasserstein distance and threshold drift are zero for every
direction \(w\), including directions with \(w^\top w_\star=0\) and hence lower
TPR. They do not identify the reproducible covariance that lets CAP select
\(w_\star\).

The same point appears geometrically in the location-shift construction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:marginal-nonidentifiability}. If
\(P_0=\Normal(0,I)\), \(P_1=\Normal(\delta w_\star,I)\), and the second
validation domain has a benign location shift along the anomaly direction,
\[
    X^{(1)}\sim\Normal(0,I),
    \qquad
    X^{(2)}\sim\Normal(\eta w_\star,I),
    \qquad
    \eta>0.
\]
For any unit-norm direction \(w\) with \(w^\top w_\star\ge0\), write
\[
    c(w)=w^\top w_\star\in[0,1].
\]
Then
\[
    \TPR_w(\alpha)
    =
    \Phi\!\left(\delta c(w)-\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\right),
    \qquad
    M_{W_1}(w)=\eta c(w),
\]
and
\[
    M_{\mathrm{thr},\alpha}(w)
    =
    1-\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-\eta c(w)\right)-\alpha,
\]
so both marginal stability baselines are strictly increasing in \(c(w)\), while
TPR is also strictly increasing in \(c(w)\). Hence, when the dimension is at
least two, every direction in
\[
    \mathcal R_\epsilon
    =
    \{w:\|w\|=1,\ 0\le c(w)\le 1-\epsilon\},
    \qquad
    \epsilon\in(0,1),
\]
has lower Wasserstein and threshold drift than \(w_\star\), even though every
such direction has strictly lower TPR than \(w_\star\). Thus marginal stability
prefers lower-power scores over the true anomaly direction.
